Why this mark works
The F
Serif letterform gives it weight and authority. This isn't a toy — it's a tool that handles your important files. The serif says "professional" without saying "corporate".
The dot ·
That small circle next to the F represents a single file — the atom of the whole system. It's subtle enough that most people won't notice it consciously, but it makes the mark feel considered.
The rounded square
Friendly, modern, app-like. Works as a Windows taskbar icon, a favicon, a desktop shortcut. Scales from 16px to 1000px without losing its identity.
The wordmark — Instrument Serif
"FileFlow" in Instrument Serif. No bold, no caps — just elegant. Sits naturally next to the mark. The tagline below in Geist Mono grounds it technically.

Colour rule: The app is black and white by default. Colour is only used to communicate status — green means safe/done, red means danger/stop, amber means caution/waiting. Never used decoratively.
05 — Motion Principles
Progress fills
The progress bar eases in, never jumps. Gives the user a sense of steady, reliable work happening.
Thinking dots
Three dots pulse when FileFlow is processing. Never a spinning circle — too anxious. Dots feel calm.
Messages fade in
Every chat message slides up gently. Never pops or flashes. The pace is calm — like a thoughtful person responding.

06 — Voice & Tone

FileFlow speaks like a calm, competent colleague — not a robot, not a chatbot, not a wizard. It never uses technical language unless it has to. It always tells you what it found before it asks what to do.

✗ Never say this
"Executing recursive directory traversal..."
"Error: FileNotFoundException at path C:\..."
"Initialising deduplication algorithm"
"Operation completed successfully. 0 errors."
"Warning: Potential file conflict detected"
✓ Always say this
"Scanning your Downloads folder — just looking, nothing moves yet."
"I couldn't access one file — it might be open in another program. I skipped it."
"I found 312 files that look like duplicates. Want me to show you before doing anything?"
"All done! Here's what I moved and where it went."
"Two of these files look the same. Which one do you want to keep?"
